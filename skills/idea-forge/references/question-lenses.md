# Question Lenses

Select lenses by uncertainty and consequence. Do not ask every question.

## Problem truth

- What observable problem exists without describing the proposed solution?
- Who experiences it, how often, and what do they do today?
- What evidence separates a painful problem from an interesting inconvenience?
- If nothing changes, who pays the cost?

Crack signal: the idea is a solution searching for a problem or the actor cannot be named precisely.

## Actor and value exchange

- Are user, buyer, payer, approver, operator, and beneficiary different?
- What action or resource does each party contribute?
- Who receives value and who absorbs risk, work, or loss of control?
- What incentive causes adoption, repeated use, and continued payment or support?

Crack signal: the beneficiary is not the decision maker and no credible incentive bridges them.

## Existing behavior and alternatives

- What is the real alternative: a competitor, spreadsheet, staff time, doing nothing, or a workaround?
- Why would someone switch, and what transition cost resists the change?
- Which current behavior must change for the idea to work?
- What advantage remains after incumbents copy the visible feature?

Crack signal: the proposal compares itself with an idealized empty market.

## Feasibility and operations

- What is the hardest technical, data, legal, or organizational dependency?
- What must be true at scale that is easy in a demo?
- Who operates, supports, audits, and recovers the system?
- Which inputs are unavailable, low quality, permissioned, or expensive?

Crack signal: feasibility depends on unowned ongoing work or data the project cannot lawfully or reliably obtain.

## Failure and misuse

- How does the idea fail quietly, partially, or at the worst time?
- Who can misuse, game, over-trust, or be excluded by it?
- What harm is externalized to a non-user?
- What happens when automation is wrong, unavailable, or adversarially manipulated?

Crack signal: the happy path creates unacceptable downside without detection, appeal, or recovery.

## Distribution and durability

- How does the first credible user discover and trust it?
- Which channel, integration, or existing relationship supplies distribution?
- Does growth improve the product, increase cost, or erode quality?
- What remains difficult, valuable, or trusted after the feature is copied?

Crack signal: the plan assumes discovery or trust appears after construction.

## Constraint removal and shape

- Which constraint is physical, contractual, policy, compatibility, budget, or merely assumed?
- If one assumed constraint disappeared, what simpler idea would remain?
- Is the current form the smallest mechanism that delivers the value?
- Can a manual, narrower, local, or reversible version test the premise?

Crack signal: the proposed implementation is carrying assumptions that are not part of the value.

## Commitment and reversibility

- What commitment becomes expensive to undo?
- What is the cheapest evidence that could change the pursue/stop decision?
- What result would kill the idea, not just suggest iteration?
- What must be learned before planning, and what can wait until after a pilot?

Crack signal: every possible experiment result is interpreted as permission to continue.
