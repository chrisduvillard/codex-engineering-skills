# Decision Record Contract

Lead with the outcome. Keep the record concise enough to re-evaluate quickly.

## Required structure

1. **Decision**
   - Recommendation, owner or audience, date, horizon, and high, medium, or low confidence.
   - One-sentence explanation of why this option wins now.

2. **Requirements frame**
   - Hard gates with their authority.
   - Weighted preferences and weights.
   - Unknown or disputed constraints.

3. **Candidates and screen**
   - Status quo and credible alternatives considered.
   - Eliminated candidates with gate, evidence, and reason.

4. **Evidence matrix**
   - One row per decisive criterion.
   - Observed evidence before any score.
   - Citations beside current external claims.
   - Unknown, disputed, and stale cells remain visibly marked.

5. **Economics and exit**
   - Total cost over the decision horizon.
   - Integration and operational burden.
   - Lock-in, migration-away cost, and ownership boundary.

6. **Sensitivity and challenge**
   - Weights or assumptions that can change the winner.
   - Strongest argument against the recommendation.
   - Runner-up and exact conditions under which it wins.

7. **Reversibility plan**
   - Cheapest hedge or discriminating pilot.
   - Rollback, export, or exit test.
   - Revisit triggers, decision expiry, and claims that age fastest.

8. **Residual uncertainty**
   - Evidence not found or unsafe to obtain.
   - Unverified claims the recommendation still depends on.
   - Smallest next check for each consequential gap.

9. **Sources**
   - Direct source list with publisher, publication or version date, access date, and claim supported.

## Verdict language

- Say **recommend** when one option clears every gate and remains ahead under plausible sensitivity.
- Say **conditional recommendation** when the winner depends on an unresolved assumption or narrow weight range.
- Say **pilot first** when a cheap experiment can discriminate better than further desk research.
- Say **no decision** when no candidate clears the frame or the user must resolve a policy choice.

Do not say "best" without naming the frame and horizon. Do not report a precise aggregate score without the underlying evidence and weights.

Do not invent intermediate confidence labels such as "medium-high." Choose the lower supported level and state what evidence would upgrade it.
